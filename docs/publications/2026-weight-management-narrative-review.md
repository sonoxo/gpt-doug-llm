## Abstract

Obesity is a chronic disease for which effective management increasingly spans behavioral intervention, pharmacotherapy, and metabolic or bariatric surgery. This narrative review summarizes representative high-quality evidence on body-weight outcomes from intensive lifestyle intervention, contemporary anti-obesity medications, investigational incretin-based agents, and surgical treatment, with emphasis on interpreting results within their original study designs rather than as direct cross-trial rankings. In the Look AHEAD trial, intensive lifestyle intervention produced approximately 9% mean weight loss at 1 year and nearly 5% maintained loss at 8 years among adults with overweight or obesity and type 2 diabetes. Phase 3 trials of tirzepatide, higher-dose semaglutide, and oral orforglipron have reported double-digit mean reductions in body weight in selected populations, while metabolic surgery remains associated with average long-term losses commonly in the 15%-30% range depending on procedure and cohort. Investigational agents such as retatrutide and cagrilintide-semaglutide have produced substantial trial reductions but, as of September 21, 2026, retatrutide and cagrilintide are not components of FDA-approved drugs. The evidence supports a multidisciplinary model in which fitness professionals contribute to physical activity, resistance training, functional capacity, and behavior support while diagnosis, medication selection, and surgical decisions remain within licensed medical practice. The central practical conclusion is that meaningful weight management should be evaluated by sustained health, function, and risk reduction rather than scale change alone.

**Keywords:** obesity; weight management; lifestyle intervention; anti-obesity medication; GLP-1; bariatric surgery; exercise; fitness professional

## 1. Introduction

Obesity management has changed rapidly. Lifestyle treatment remains foundational, but pharmacologic and surgical options can now produce levels of average weight reduction that were uncommon in older nonsurgical programs. For practitioners and the public, this creates an interpretation problem: percentages reported by different studies are often compared as though they came from one head-to-head experiment, even when the populations, durations, estimands, adherence assumptions, and treatment settings differ.

This review therefore focuses on representative outcomes and the boundaries of what those outcomes mean. It does not rank therapies. Instead, it separates evidence by intervention class, regulatory status, and clinical context, and it highlights where a fitness professional can appropriately contribute within a multidisciplinary care model.

## 2. Methods: Targeted Narrative Evidence Review

A targeted narrative search was performed for landmark randomized trials and authoritative U.S. regulatory or federal health sources relevant to adult weight management. Priority was given to peer-reviewed randomized trials in major medical journals, FDA approval and safety communications, and National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) summaries of long-term lifestyle and bariatric-surgery evidence. Sources were reviewed through September 21, 2026.

This was not a systematic review or meta-analysis. No pooled effect estimate was calculated, and cross-trial percentages are not treated as comparative efficacy rankings. Patent publications and commercial claims were excluded as evidence of clinical efficacy; they may identify inventions or hypotheses but do not substitute for randomized clinical trials, regulatory review, or peer-reviewed clinical evidence.

## 3. Intensive Lifestyle Intervention

Behavioral treatment remains a core component of weight management and is frequently used alongside medication or surgery. In the NIH-supported Look AHEAD study, adults with overweight or obesity and type 2 diabetes assigned to an intensive lifestyle intervention lost about 9% of their starting weight after 1 year. At 8 years, the intervention group maintained nearly 5% mean weight loss, compared with about 2% in the diabetes support and education group. These results demonstrate both the potential and the difficulty of sustaining substantial lifestyle-associated loss over many years.

The clinical value of lifestyle treatment is broader than weight change alone. Physical activity, cardiorespiratory fitness, strength, mobility, sleep, dietary quality, and the capacity to sustain health behaviors remain relevant outcomes even when scale change is modest. This is especially important when lifestyle support is combined with more potent medical therapies.

## 4. Contemporary Anti-Obesity Pharmacotherapy

Tirzepatide, a dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist, produced large body-weight reductions in the SURMOUNT-1 trial. At 72 weeks, mean percentage changes in body weight were -15.0%, -19.5%, and -20.9% with 5 mg, 10 mg, and 15 mg doses, respectively, compared with -3.1% with placebo. The FDA approved tirzepatide (Zepbound) for chronic weight management in 2023 in combination with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity. Long-term follow-up in participants with obesity and prediabetes further demonstrated sustained weight reduction over 176 weeks, while reinforcing the chronic-treatment nature of obesity pharmacotherapy.

Semaglutide has also advanced. The STEP UP phase 3b trial evaluated once-weekly semaglutide 7.2 mg in adults with obesity. Published results reported approximately 18.7% mean weight reduction at 72 weeks under the treatment-policy estimand, with larger reductions under an efficacy/on-treatment analysis. On March 19, 2026, the FDA approved the new 7.2-mg dose, called Wegovy HD, for weight loss and long-term maintenance of weight loss in certain adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related condition.

Orforglipron, an oral nonpeptide GLP-1 receptor agonist, was studied in the ATTAIN-1 phase 3 trial. At 72 weeks, mean body-weight change was -7.5%, -8.4%, and -11.2% with 6 mg, 12 mg, and 36 mg doses, respectively, compared with -2.1% with placebo. The FDA approved orforglipron (Foundayo) on April 1, 2026 for long-term weight reduction in eligible adults, combined with reduced-calorie intake and increased physical activity.

These medications differ in route, mechanism, dose, adverse-effect profile, contraindications, trial population, and regulatory labeling. Percent weight loss from separate trials should therefore not be interpreted as a direct ranking. Treatment choice requires individualized medical assessment.

## 5. Investigational Agents and the Importance of Regulatory Status

Investigational therapies can generate striking trial results before regulatory approval. In a phase 2 trial, the triple-receptor agonist retatrutide produced mean weight reductions ranging from -8.7% to -24.2% at 48 weeks across studied dose groups, compared with -2.1% with placebo. In the phase 3a REDEFINE 1 trial, coadministered cagrilintide and semaglutide produced an estimated mean weight reduction of -20.4% at 68 weeks compared with -3.0% with placebo.

As of September 21, 2026, FDA communications state that retatrutide and cagrilintide are not components of FDA-approved drugs and have not been found safe and effective for any condition by the agency. Accordingly, published trial findings should not be converted into consumer claims that these products are approved treatments, nor should unapproved compounded or direct-to-consumer versions be represented as equivalent to products reviewed by the FDA.

## 6. Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery

Metabolic and bariatric surgery remains an important treatment option for appropriately selected patients. NIDDK summarizes evidence showing that many patients lose on average about 15%-30% of their starting body weight, depending on procedure. In the Longitudinal Assessment of Bariatric Surgery (LABS), average weight loss at 7 years was about 28% of starting weight after gastric bypass and about 15% after adjustable gastric banding.

These outcomes should not be directly compared with medication trials without considering differences in eligibility, baseline disease burden, treatment exposure, follow-up, complications, and study design. Surgery also carries procedure-specific risks and requires long-term nutritional and medical follow-up.

## 8. The Role of Fitness Professionals

Fitness professionals can add value to obesity care without functioning as prescribers or diagnosticians. Appropriate contributions include exercise programming within scope of practice, progressive resistance training, aerobic conditioning, movement confidence, functional-capacity development, adherence support, and coordination with licensed clinicians when medical symptoms, medication adverse effects, or contraindications arise.

The expansion of effective anti-obesity medication also increases the importance of preserving physical function during weight reduction. Weight loss includes both fat mass and lean tissue. Resistance exercise, adequate nutrition under appropriate professional guidance, and ongoing monitoring of strength and function may therefore be clinically meaningful complements to medically supervised weight reduction. The trainer role is supportive and collaborative; medication selection, diagnosis, treatment of obesity-related disease, and surgical decisions remain medical responsibilities.

## 9. Limitations

This is a selective narrative review, not a systematic review, and it does not include every approved medication, every relevant trial, or a formal risk-of-bias assessment. Results from separate trials use different estimands and populations. The review therefore avoids comparative rankings and should not be used to infer that one intervention is universally superior to another.

Regulatory status can change. Statements regarding FDA approval are current through September 21, 2026 and should be rechecked at the time of clinical use or subsequent republication.

## 10. Conclusion

The contemporary weight-management evidence base supports a spectrum of interventions rather than a single universal solution. Intensive lifestyle treatment can produce clinically meaningful and durable benefit; modern anti-obesity medications can produce substantial average weight reductions in selected patients; and metabolic surgery remains a powerful long-term option for appropriate candidates. Investigational agents continue to extend the research frontier but must be clearly separated from approved treatment.

For fitness professionals, the strongest role is not to replace medical care but to improve the physical-activity, strength, function, and behavior-support components that help patients translate weight reduction into durable health.
