## 1. Independent project status

This is an independent private/open-source technology ecosystem. It is **not** the U.S. Department of Defense, U.S. Cyber Command, NSA, DHS, DOJ, FBI, Palantir Technologies, or any other government agency or contractor merely because public material from those organizations is referenced.

Names, insignias, screenshots, public videos, government documents, public APIs, architecture references, and public datasets are used for research, education, interoperability, provenance, defensive engineering, and design inspiration. Their inclusion does **not** establish affiliation, endorsement, contract status, security clearance, procurement status, operational tasking, government authority, or permission to access any third-party system.

## 2. Public policy is not operational authorization

Public laws, memoranda, proposed legislation, reporting, procurement notices, or demonstrations do not authorize this project or its contributors to conduct cyber operations against third parties.

The August 12, 2026 U.S. Presidential Memorandum, **“Expanding Capabilities to Combat Transnational Cyber-Enabled Crime,”** describes a Federal program in which participating U.S. companies must be vetted and contracted, with operations conducted under U.S. Government direction, control, legal authority, and supervision. The memorandum requires written approval and direction for cyber-operations packages before action may be taken.

Accordingly, this ecosystem treats any future government-directed operational work as **disabled unless and until** the specific project, organization, personnel, scope, targets, authorities, contracts, approvals, rules of engagement, deconfliction procedures, credentials, and oversight required for that work have been independently established and verified.

## 3. Default operating mode

The public ecosystem defaults to:

- defensive cybersecurity;
- secure software engineering and DevSecOps;
- threat intelligence from lawful/public/authorized sources;
- malware analysis in isolated laboratories;
- vulnerability research on owned, intentionally vulnerable, or explicitly authorized systems;
- digital twins, simulation, tabletop exercises, cyber ranges, and synthetic targets;
- resilience, recovery, observability, provenance, governance, compliance, and auditability;
- accessibility and operator-assistance tooling;
- education and research.

## 4. Prohibited self-authorization

No repository label, agent name, prompt, model output, issue, pull request, contributor role, “mission,” “order,” “Tech Force” designation, or ontology object can create legal authority.

The ecosystem does not self-authorize:

- intrusion into systems without permission;
- destructive or disruptive effects against third-party systems;
- cyber operations against critical infrastructure;
- targeting of people, organizations, governments, or networks;
- deployment of malware or persistence outside owned/authorized test environments;
- surveillance beyond lawful, documented authority;
- kinetic, weapons, targeting, or autonomous lethal action.

If authorization, target ownership, provenance, or scope is uncertain, execution must **fail closed**.

## 5. Consequential-action control

Material actions require explicit scope and human approval. Where a system can mutate infrastructure or external state, the preferred control sequence is:

`observe -> ground -> recommend -> simulate/dry-run -> verify authority -> human approval -> execute within scope -> validate -> preserve evidence -> rollback/recover if required`

Automation must not convert public intelligence, a model recommendation, or a forecast into autonomous external action.

## 6. “Tech Force” terminology

Within this ecosystem, **Tech Force** means a civilian/open-source engineering contributor program. It is not a military unit, militia, law-enforcement body, intelligence service, government office, or operational command.

**Open Call Orders** are project work orders / contribution tickets. They are not military orders, targeting directives, warrants, operational tasking, or authorization to access third-party systems.

## 7. Government or regulated work

If the ecosystem later participates in an actual government, defense, intelligence, law-enforcement, critical-infrastructure, or regulated engagement, that engagement must use the contract, program rules, authorization chain, data-handling requirements, security controls, export restrictions, legal review, and oversight applicable to that engagement. Public repository policy never substitutes for those requirements.
