## 1. Independent project status

This is an independent private/open-source technology ecosystem. It is **not** the U.S. Department of Defense/Department of War, U.S. Cyber Command, NSA, DHS, DOJ, FBI, Palantir Technologies, or any other government agency or contractor merely because public material from those organizations is referenced.

Names, insignias, screenshots, public videos, government documents, public APIs, architecture references, and public datasets are used for research, education, interoperability, provenance, defensive engineering, and design inspiration. Their inclusion does **not** establish affiliation, endorsement, contract status, security clearance, procurement status, operational tasking, government authority, or permission to access any third-party system.

## 2. Public policy is not operational authorization

Public laws, memoranda, proposed legislation, reporting, procurement notices, or demonstrations do not authorize this project or its contributors to conduct cyber operations against third parties.

The August 12, 2026 U.S. Presidential Memorandum, **“Expanding Capabilities to Combat Transnational Cyber-Enabled Crime,”** describes a Federal program in which participating U.S. companies must be accepted into the program, vetted, and contracted, with operations conducted under U.S. Government direction, control, legal authority, and supervision. The memorandum requires operating procedures, written review/approval and direction for every cyber-operations package before action, continued-participation review, and notification/minimization procedures for activity exceeding approved parameters.

Accordingly, this ecosystem treats any future government-directed operational work as **disabled unless and until** the specific project, organization, personnel, scope, targets, authorities, contracts, approvals, rules of engagement, deconfliction procedures, credentials, and oversight required for that work have been independently established and verified.

## 3. Current Federal cyber/cryptography policy references

### National Security Systems

NSPM-12, dated June 12, 2026, establishes cybersecurity governance for National Security Systems, re-establishes the Committee on National Security Systems, and identifies the NSA Director as National Manager for NSS. It rescinds NSD-42 and NSM-8. Public references to NSS governance do **not** confer NSS designation, access, CNSS membership, National Manager authority, clearance, accreditation, or government status on this ecosystem.

### Post-quantum cryptography

Executive Order 14412, **“Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks,”** dated June 22, 2026, establishes Federal policy for migration to NIST-approved FIPS post-quantum cryptography and directs Federal migration milestones. It also directs CISA/NIST assistance for critical-infrastructure owners and operators and calls for proposed FAR changes affecting covered contractors. The order does **not** itself make TECH FORCE a Federal contractor or impose Federal-system requirements on this public repository.

OMB M-26-15, dated June 24, 2026, implements the Federal PQC migration effort for executive agencies and expressly excludes National Security Systems.

TECH FORCE may perform local cryptographic inventory, algorithm-agility testing, PQC migration simulation, key-management documentation, and software cryptographic dependency mapping. Such work is informational/engineering work and is **not** FIPS validation, Federal approval, CMMC assessment, NSS authorization, or a government cryptographic certification.

### Defense AI cybersecurity

Public DoD CIO AI cybersecurity guidance states that DoD AI systems must undergo applicable cybersecurity assessment and authorization under the Risk Management Framework. TECH FORCE references to this material are implementation-mapping references only; they do not constitute DoD authorization or an ATO.

## 5. Prohibited self-authorization

No repository label, agent name, prompt, model output, issue, pull request, contributor role, “mission,” “order,” Tech Force designation, or ontology object can create legal authority.

The ecosystem does not self-authorize:

- intrusion into systems without permission;
- destructive or disruptive effects against third-party systems;
- cyber operations against critical infrastructure;
- targeting of people, organizations, governments, or networks;
- deployment of malware or persistence outside owned/authorized test environments;
- surveillance beyond lawful, documented authority;
- collection of third-party cryptographic keys or decryption of protected third-party traffic;
- cryptographic or security testing of Federal, NSS, DoD, or critical-infrastructure systems without explicit authorization;
- kinetic, weapons, targeting, or autonomous lethal action.

If authorization, target ownership, provenance, or scope is uncertain, execution must **fail closed**.

## 6. Consequential-action control

Material actions require explicit scope and human approval. Where a system can mutate infrastructure or external state, the preferred control sequence is:

`observe -> ground -> recommend -> simulate/dry-run -> verify authority -> human approval -> execute within scope -> validate -> preserve evidence -> rollback/recover if required`

Automation must not convert public intelligence, a model recommendation, or a forecast into autonomous external action.

Within this ecosystem, **Tech Force** means a civilian/open-source engineering contributor program. It is not a military unit, militia, law-enforcement body, intelligence service, government office, or operational command.

**Open Call Orders** are project work orders / contribution tickets. They are not military orders, targeting directives, warrants, operational tasking, or authorization to access third-party systems.
